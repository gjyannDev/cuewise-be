# UX design and engineering collaboration

## From requirements to user flows
Probe how they turn a vague brief into flows, wireframes and a prototype, and what they ask before drawing anything. Strong: states the user's goal and the constraints first, shows the steps a user takes, and checks the prototype against the brief. Weak: jumps to visual polish. Follow-up: what do you do when the requirements conflict with what users need?

## User research that changes a design
Probe a decision they changed because of research: how they chose the method, who they spoke to, what they learned. Strong: names the evidence and the design change it caused. Weak: describes research that confirmed what they already believed. Follow-up: how do you decide when you have enough data to act?

## Usability testing and reporting findings
Probe how they run a usability session and write it up: tasks, observed problems, impact and a proposed fix. Strong: ranks problems by how many users hit them and how badly, and separates what they saw from what they guess. Weak: lists opinions with no evidence. Follow-up: how do you handle a finding the team disagrees with?

## Checking feasibility with engineers
Probe how they find out a design can be built before it is final: prototype, early review, and changing it after feedback. Strong: brings engineers in early and trades a detail for delivery without losing the user goal. Weak: hands over a finished design and defends it. Follow-up: what do you do when the cost is much higher than expected?

## Clear wording in forms and instructions
Probe how question and instruction wording affects the data and the user's success: unclear options, double questions, leading text. Strong: gives a concrete example of a badly worded question and how they tested the fix. Weak: treats copy as an afterthought. Follow-up: how do you spot a confusing instruction before launch?

## Measuring whether a design worked
Probe which measures show a flow is successful: completion rate, time on task, errors, drop-off. Strong: sets the measure before launch and compares it afterwards. Weak: calls it a success because people liked it. Follow-up: what do you do when the numbers improve but users complain?
