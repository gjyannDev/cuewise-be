# Design fundamentals

## Visual hierarchy and layout
Probe how they guide the eye on a screen: size, contrast, spacing, alignment and grouping. Strong: explains why the most important action stands out and how a grid keeps screens consistent. Weak: talks about colours they like. Follow-up: a screen feels cluttered; what do you change first?

## Accessibility basics
Probe how they make a design usable for more people: colour contrast, text size, focus states, labels and not relying on colour alone. Strong: checks contrast and keyboard use as part of the work, not at the end. Weak: treats accessibility as a later phase. Follow-up: how would you design an error message for someone who cannot see red?

## User flows and wireframes
Probe how they go from a task a user wants to do to the screens that support it. Strong: maps the steps and the edge cases (empty, error, loading) before drawing high-fidelity screens. Weak: designs one happy-path screen. Follow-up: where would a user most likely give up in this flow?

## Taking critique
Probe a time their design was criticised: what they heard, what they changed and what they kept. Strong: separates the problem from the suggested fix and explains choices with the user's goal. Weak: defends every detail or changes everything. Follow-up: what do you do when two reviewers disagree?

## Handing a design to developers
Probe what they give developers so the build matches the design: specs, states, spacing, assets, and answers to questions. Strong: covers every state and checks the built result. Weak: shares a single mockup and moves on. Follow-up: a developer says your design is too hard to build; what next?
